Elo vs the fixed opponents

Strength of each evaluated checkpoint against the rules bot and SearchAI, which never change (0 = even, −200 ≈ wins 24%, +200 ≈ wins 76%). Shaded = 95% range. Dashed lines = logged changes.
How to read this: higher is better. A line that climbs over several evals is progress; one jump that stays inside the shaded range is noise.

Training games vs fixed opponents

Self-play games where the net played a fixed opponent (selfplay.opponents), from the net's side, over the last 6 and 24 hours.
How to read this: the dot is the net's win rate, the bar is where the true rate most likely lies. Right of the 50% line = the net wins more than it loses. A 6 h dot left of the 24 h dot means it is getting worse.

Beats previous checkpoint?

Each eval's score against "best", the checkpoint evaluated before it (a moving opponent, so not plotted as Elo). Bar = 95% range.
How to read this: a dot above the 50% line means the new checkpoint beat the one before it. With 40 games most bars cross 50%, so treat it as a sanity check; the fixed opponents above are the progress measure.

Value loss

How well the net predicts who wins (mean squared error, lower is better). Faint = raw, solid = smoothed.
How to read this: watch the unseen-games line: it should creep down towards the 0.67 floor. The training line far below it only shows the net remembering games it has trained on.

Policy loss

Cross-entropy against the search's improved policy. Faint = raw, solid = smoothed.
How to read this: lower = the net predicts the search's choice of move better. The unseen-games line is the honest one; a widening gap below it means memorising.

Policy entropy

How spread out the net's move choices are. Faint = raw, solid = smoothed.
How to read this: a slow fall means the net is getting more decisive. A drop to about 0 in the first hours means collapse: lower train.lr.

Self-play throughput

Training positions generated per hour, over hours since the run started.
How to read this: flat is healthy. Gaps are pauses or restarts; a lasting drop means slower games (engine, opponent mix) or fewer live workers.

Latest eval by the net's deck

Wins-losses-draws for each deck the net played, per opponent.

Changes
